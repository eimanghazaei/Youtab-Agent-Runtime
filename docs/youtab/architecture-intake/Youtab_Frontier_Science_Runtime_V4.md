# Document Status and Position

This document defines V4 of the Youtab scientific intelligence architecture. V4 does not replace V1, V2, or V3. It sits above them as a frontier-science specialization layer for astrophysics, quantum science, scientific perception, probabilistic inference, high-performance simulation, and domain-specific verification.

```text
V1 = Advanced Computation Runtime
V2 = Research / Critical-Grade Computation Runtime
V3 = Certified Scientific Intelligence and Experimental Governance Runtime
V4 = Frontier Science Runtime for Astrophysics, Quantum, Scientific Perception, and Domain-Specialized Research
```

V4 is required when the task is no longer merely a mathematical or physical calculation, but a domain-specific scientific reasoning problem involving real instruments, observational data, quantum states, astrophysical models, noisy measurements, multi-scale simulation, theory selection, and publication-grade evidence.

# Core Doctrine

The core doctrine of V4 is that frontier science cannot be solved by a single language model. It requires a governed system of models, tools, solvers, simulators, data pipelines, vision/perception engines, inference engines, verification layers, uncertainty models, and human-controlled autonomy.

```text
LLM coordinates.
Domain runtime formalizes.
Scientific data grounds.
Solver computes.
Simulator predicts.
Inference estimates.
Perception extracts evidence.
Verifier challenges.
Authority governs.
Trace proves.
Human stops, approves, or constrains when risk requires it.
```

A model may propose, explain, route, critique, and synthesize. It must not become the final source of scientific truth for high-risk or research-grade claims. Scientific truth status comes from evidence, reproducibility, formal verification when possible, calibrated instruments, validated simulations, accepted domain methods, and governed review.

# Domain Ontology Layer

The Domain Ontology Layer turns scientific text, equations, data, and questions into governed objects. Without this layer, the system risks confusing similar words with valid scientific equivalence.

- Represent physical quantities with units, dimensions, symbols, uncertainty, measurement source, and validity domain.
- Represent equations with assumptions, derivation context, allowed regimes, boundary conditions, and known failure cases.
- Represent scientific models as explicit objects: Newtonian, relativistic, quantum, semi-classical, thermodynamic, fluid, cosmological, empirical, statistical, or numerical.
- Represent observables separately from latent variables. A telescope image, a spectrum, a detector count, and a posterior estimate are not the same type of evidence.
- Represent instruments as evidence-producing systems with calibration status, noise model, bias model, resolution, field of view, sampling rate, and provenance.

This ontology must connect to Knowledge Governance. A retrieved formula is not automatically valid; it is valid only inside its regime and under its assumptions.

The Astrophysics Runtime is required for scientific tasks involving astronomy, cosmology, telescope data, spectra, celestial mechanics, orbital modeling, gravitational systems, radiation, light curves, and large-scale structure.

- Observational pipeline: ingest FITS-like scientific images, time-series, spectra, telescope metadata, calibration frames, coordinate systems, and instrument models.
- Spectral intelligence: line detection, redshift estimation, continuum fitting, absorption/emission feature identification, uncertainty-aware classification.
- Time-domain intelligence: light-curve extraction, periodicity analysis, transient detection, variability modeling, anomaly detection, event candidate ranking.
- Celestial mechanics: orbital propagation, N-body simulation, perturbation analysis, coordinate transformations, frame consistency checks.
- Cosmology and large-scale modeling: model comparison, parameter estimation, likelihood construction, posterior inference, theory-regime declaration.
- Radiative transfer and plasma-aware modeling: treat radiation transport, opacity, temperature, density, emission, absorption, and scattering as model-governed scientific objects.
- Observational humility: distinguish observation, inferred property, model-dependent interpretation, and speculative explanation.

# Quantum Runtime

The Quantum Runtime is required for quantum mechanics, quantum information, quantum computing, quantum chemistry, condensed matter, many-body physics, and open quantum systems. It must not reduce quantum reasoning to verbal explanation. It must formalize states, operators, spaces, measurements, approximations, and computational methods.

- State representation: vectors, density matrices, wavefunctions, basis choices, normalization, phase conventions, and uncertainty.
- Operator governance: Hermiticity, commutators, eigenstates, observables, Hamiltonians, time evolution, measurement operators.
- Quantum circuit runtime: circuit construction, gate decomposition, simulator routing, noise modeling, measurement sampling, error mitigation.
- Open-system runtime: decoherence, Lindblad models, noise channels, thermal environments, dissipative dynamics.
- Quantum chemistry runtime: electronic structure, basis sets, approximations, variational methods, correlation treatment, molecule provenance.
- Many-body runtime: tensor networks, exact diagonalization, mean-field approximations, DMRG-like workflows, entanglement measures.
- Quantum epistemic caution: separate exact theorem, simulated result, hardware sample, noisy estimate, and interpretation.

| Quantum Task | Primary Engines | Required Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Textbook quantum problem | CAS, linear algebra engine, domain RAG | Normalization, eigenvalue/eigenvector check, operator domain check. |
| Circuit simulation | Quantum circuit simulator, noise model | Shot uncertainty, simulator/backend declaration, gate-set consistency. |
| Open quantum system | ODE solver, Lindblad/open-system engine | Trace preservation, positivity checks, convergence and timestep checks. |
| Quantum chemistry | Electronic structure engine, basis registry | Basis-set declaration, convergence, method limitations, reference checks. |
| Many-body problem | Tensor network/exact diagonalization/specialized solver | Finite-size effects, truncation error, benchmark limits, symmetry checks. |

# Probabilistic Inference Runtime

V4 must treat uncertainty as a first-class artifact. A frontier-science answer without uncertainty is incomplete. The system must distinguish measurement uncertainty, model uncertainty, numerical uncertainty, statistical uncertainty, instrument uncertainty, approximation uncertainty, and epistemic uncertainty.

- Build likelihood models explicitly and record assumptions.
- Record priors, posterior distributions, posterior predictive checks, convergence diagnostics, and sensitivity to priors.
- Use Bayesian inference, MCMC, variational inference, ensemble methods, and Monte Carlo propagation where appropriate.
- Distinguish aleatoric uncertainty from epistemic uncertainty.
- When uncertainty cannot be quantified, label the output as unquantified or insufficiently constrained instead of presenting false precision.

| Inference Object | Meaning | V4 Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prior | What was assumed before data | Must be explicit, justified, and sensitivity-tested when important. |
| Likelihood | How data is generated under a model | Must reflect noise and measurement process, not only fitted values. |
| Posterior | Updated state after evidence | Must include uncertainty, not just point estimate. |
| Posterior predictive check | Model realism check | Required for research-grade inference. |
| Model evidence/comparison | Compare theories or model families | Required when multiple plausible scientific models exist. |

# Multi-Scale and Multi-Fidelity Simulation Governance

V4 must avoid using the most expensive simulation for every task while also avoiding cheap approximations for critical work. It must route through a model ladder.

```text
Low-fidelity analytical estimate
        ↓
Medium-fidelity numerical model
        ↓
High-fidelity simulation
        ↓
Calibration with real data
        ↓
Validated predictive model
        ↓
Certified result only when gate conditions are met
```

- Every fidelity level must declare assumptions and expected error.
- Escalation must be driven by risk, user requirement, uncertainty, sensitivity, and evidence gap.
- High-fidelity simulation must include convergence tests and validation targets.
- Surrogate models may be used for speed, but only with validity envelopes and out-of-distribution detection.
- For critical claims, reduced-order models cannot replace high-fidelity verification unless explicitly validated.

# Instrument-Aware Data Pipeline

Scientific data is not raw truth. It is produced by instruments under conditions, calibration, resolution limits, noise, bias, and preprocessing choices. V4 must model the measurement process.

- Capture raw data, processed data, calibration files, instrument configuration, timestamps, operator notes, environmental conditions, and provenance.
- Preserve data lineage from raw observation to derived result.
- Record preprocessing steps: denoising, filtering, normalization, cropping, stacking, background subtraction, alignment, interpolation, and outlier handling.
- Maintain uncertainty propagation through preprocessing and analysis.
- Flag when data quality is insufficient for a claim.

For laboratory and camera-connected workflows, Youtab may automate ingestion, monitoring, measurement extraction, and analysis inside delegated scope. It must remain stoppable and auditable.

# Theory-Model-Data Alignment

V4 must prevent scientific category errors. A result can be numerically precise and scientifically wrong if the wrong model family is used.

- Declare the theory family: Newtonian, relativistic, quantum, quantum field, thermodynamic, fluid, cosmological, empirical, statistical, or effective theory.
- Check validity regime: velocity, scale, temperature, density, energy, coupling strength, continuum assumption, weak/strong field, open/closed system.
- Check that the data actually measures the quantity being inferred.
- Check that the chosen model is not underdetermined by the data.
- When multiple models fit the data, report alternatives and model comparison status.

The final answer must state whether it is a direct calculation, a model-dependent inference, a simulation result, an experimentally supported result, or a formally verified claim.

# Frontier Science Safety and Controlled Autonomy

V4 must preserve powerful positive autonomy for agents and AI inside the authority delegated by humans. The system should not be over-restricted in ordinary scientific work. It must perform strongly, automatically, and creatively inside the granted task scope. Control is required when work becomes safety-critical, legally constrained, externally side-effecting, or capable of causing harm.

- If the human delegates a limited task, the agent must stay within that limited scope.
- If the human grants broad automatic execution authority, the agent may use all granted permissions to complete the objective, including planning, tool selection, simulations, analysis, and iterative improvement.
- No delegated autonomy may bypass law, safety policy, human stop controls, approval gates, or Youtab Cognitive Authority.
- All autonomous work must be traceable, auditable, revocable, and bounded by an autonomy lease.
- For safety-critical or regulated actions, the agent must request human approval before execution or before final certification.
- The human must retain pause, stop, kill, revoke, override, rescope, and re-instruct capabilities.
- If control is lost, monitoring is lost, risk changes, or the autonomy lease expires, the system must fail closed or pause for review.

| Autonomy Class | Allowed Behavior | Required Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0 Advisory | Explain, calculate, retrieve, suggest. | No external side effects; normal trace. |
| A1 Bounded Automatic | Run tools and analyses inside a clearly scoped task. | Delegated scope, cost limits, trace, user-visible assumptions. |
| A2 Extended Scientific Workflow | Run iterative experiments, simulations, literature/data checks, and verification cycles. | Autonomy lease, checkpoints, cost/risk limits, stop/pause controls. |
| A3 Laboratory-Connected | Monitor instruments, ingest data, analyze camera/sensor streams, propose adjustments. | Human approval for physical changes, strict sandbox, audit trail, emergency stop. |
| A4 Safety-Critical / Regulated | Anything affecting safety, regulated systems, real equipment, or irreversible action. | Human approval required; fail-closed; no autonomous final action without authorization. |

# Model Strategy for V4

V4 must not rely on one universal model. It must use a model registry and routing strategy. Models are engines. Cognitive Authority remains the decision authority.

| Model Type | Role | Not Allowed To Do Alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fast Coordinator Model | Cheap routing, simple explanations, low-risk classification. | Final answer to high-risk scientific claims. |
| Deep Reasoning Model | Formalization, planning, multi-step reasoning, tool orchestration. | Act as proof checker or final scientific authority. |
| Domain Specialist Model | Astrophysics, quantum, chemistry, vision, simulation planning. | Override verification or safety gates. |
| Vision/Perception Model | Extract evidence from images/video/instruments. | Certify physical reality without calibration and verification. |
| Critic/Verifier Model | Challenge assumptions, find errors, detect hallucinations, check consistency. | Replace domain solvers or formal proof systems. |
| Code/Tool-Use Model | Generate and run scientific code under sandbox. | Execute unsafe code or unapproved external side effects. |
| Human Expert | Review and approve safety-critical or certification-grade outputs. | Be bypassed when policy requires approval. |

# V4 Execution Workflow

```text
1. Understand intent and required scientific domain.
2. Classify risk, autonomy class, cost class, and required evidence level.
3. Select V1/V2/V3/V4 path.
4. Formalize variables, units, assumptions, theory family, model regime, data sources, and target claim.
5. Retrieve domain knowledge, datasets, instrument metadata, and prior validated models.
6. Select specialist model(s), solver(s), perception engine(s), inference engine(s), and verification method(s).
7. Execute low-fidelity pass first unless risk or user requirement demands high fidelity immediately.
8. Escalate fidelity and compute only when needed.
9. Perform domain-specific verification, uncertainty quantification, and cross-checks.
10. Produce a claim-status-labeled answer with trace, assumptions, sources, uncertainty, and limitations.
11. Require human approval for safety-critical certification, external effects, regulated actions, or lab-control steps.
12. Store reproducibility package and evidence ledger when the output is research-grade or critical-grade.
```

This workflow ensures speed for simple work and precision for critical work. It prevents both under-computation and unnecessary over-computation.

# Non-Negotiable V4 Design Rules

- No single model is sufficient for frontier science.
- No answer is research-grade without assumptions, uncertainty, verification, and trace.
- No image/video/camera-derived measurement is accepted without calibration and evidence envelope when precision matters.
- No quantum answer is complete without state/operator/regime clarity.
- No astrophysics inference is complete without instrument/data/model uncertainty.
- No simulation claim is complete without convergence, validation, or limitation statement.
- No autonomous agent may bypass Cognitive Authority, safety, law, approval gates, or human stop controls.
- No critical-grade claim may be released as certified without dossier, review, and required gates.
- No expensive compute is used when a lower-fidelity validated route is sufficient.
- No low-fidelity shortcut is accepted when the task is high-risk, research-grade, or safety-critical.
